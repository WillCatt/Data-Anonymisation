Legal Text Anonymisation — Lite vs Pro

Two redaction pipelines, side by side, on three illustrative legal-style inputs.

Lite — DIRECT-only Pro — DIRECT + mosaic-aware QUASI
About this showcase. The pipelines are the same code as in src/anonymisation/pipeline/. Entity spans here are hand-curated for deterministic output; in production they come from the spaCy / RoBERTa NER from earlier phases. The mosaic scorer's haystack is a small synthetic corpus engineered to drive useful convergence behavior across the three samples — in deployment it would be the firm's own document corpus.
ECHR-style applications: individual complainants, demographic context, application numbers. The QUASI fingerprint (date + location + nationality + role) is what the mosaic loop has to wrestle with.

Press freedom — Pro fallback to suppression

k_target=5 · iters=2
Highly distinctive document (Russian journalist, named publication, specific date, specific location). The fingerprint never reaches k=5 in the haystack, so the iterate-until-safe loop runs out of room and falls back to full suppression. This is the converged=False case — graceful degradation rather than silent partial leakage.

Multi-party contract dispute

pseudonymise=True
Original Phase 4 demo — two distinct parties referenced by multiple surface forms each ('Maria Petrova', 'Mrs Petrova', 'Petrova'). Substring + honorific coref collapses each set to one token. Round-trip preserves the canonical form.

Original

Maria Petrova has filed a claim against Acme Holdings Ltd in the dispute concerning the contract dated 12 March 2018. Mrs Petrova, a Bulgarian national, alleges that John Smith, the company's chief financial officer, breached his fiduciary duty. Acme denies all wrongdoing and is represented by Sofia District Court. Smith has not yet responded to the allegations, while Petrova continues to seek damages of approximately €450,000.

Pseudonymised DIRECT → tokens

[PERSON_A] has filed a claim against [ORG_A] in the dispute concerning the contract dated 12 March 2018. [PERSON_A], a Bulgarian national, alleges that [PERSON_B], the company's chief financial officer, breached his fiduciary duty. [ORG_A] denies all wrongdoing and is represented by [ORG_B]. [PERSON_B] has not yet responded to the allegations, while [PERSON_A] continues to seek damages of approximately €450,000.

Vault stays with the firm

tokenoriginal
[PERSON_A]→ Maria Petrova
[ORG_A]→ Acme Holdings Ltd
[PERSON_B]→ John Smith
[ORG_B]→ Sofia District Court

Round-trip — what the LLM workflow looks like

1. The firm sends the pseudonymised document + a question to a third-party LLM
Question: Who is suing whom, and what court is involved?
2. The LLM responds, still using only the opaque tokens
Based on the redacted document, [PERSON_A] is suing [ORG_A] (represented by [ORG_B]) over a contract dispute. The defendants include [PERSON_B], the company's CFO.
3. The firm runs restore(answer, vault) locally
Based on the redacted document, Maria Petrova is suing Acme Holdings Ltd (represented by Sofia District Court) over a contract dispute. The defendants include John Smith, the company's CFO.

The LLM never saw the real names. The restoration step is pure local string replacement — no API call, no external dependency on the LLM provider's logs or retention policy.
